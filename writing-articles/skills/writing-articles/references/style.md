## Voice

Write like the author explaining something to a capable colleague: direct, specific,
willing to give a verdict. Use the form of address and point of view from the context
file and keep them consistent across the article.

State opinions as opinions and back them with a reason. "I would not use X for this,
because Y" beats "X may not be ideal in some cases".

## Sentences

- One idea per sentence. Most sentences under about 25 words.
- Concrete over abstract: name the tool, the number, the error message.
- Active voice, a real subject. "The build fails" not "failures may be experienced".
- Cut words that carry nothing: "basically", "actually", "in order to", "it is
  important to note that".
- Paragraphs of two to four sentences. A wall of text loses the skimmer; a list of
  fragments loses the argument.

## Patterns that read as machine-written

Remove these in every draft:

- Contrast reveals: "It's not X, it's Y", "not just X, but Y". State Y with its reason.
- Triplets by default: three adjectives, three examples, three clauses, when one or two
  would do.
- Empty openers: "In today's fast-paced world", "When it comes to", "Let's dive in".
- Questions that label your own point: "The result? Faster builds."
- Hedged nothing: "can potentially help to improve". Say what it does, or cut it.
- Closing summaries that restate the article, and "In conclusion".
- Inflated words: "leverage", "robust", "seamless", "game-changer", "unlock", "delve",
  "navigate the landscape", and their equivalents in other languages.
- Em dashes as general punctuation. Use a period, a comma, or a colon.
- Bold sprinkled through sentences. Bold the one term a skimmer must catch, if any.

## Numbers, names, and dates

- Write product names as their makers do.
- Give numbers with units and context: "about 4 GB of RAM for builds", not "lots of
  memory".
- Dates that may go stale get the month and year they were checked.
- Round where precision is false. "About a third" is honest; "33.7%" from a vendor
  survey is not.

## German

- Keep the form of address from the context file ("Sie" or "du") throughout,
  including in components and calls to action.
- Typographic quotes („...“), the decimal comma, and a space before units (4 GB).
- Use an established German term when one exists; keep English where German
  developers say the English word (Deployment, Pull Request) and do not alternate.
- Gender-inclusive language as the context file says; if it says nothing, prefer
  neutral wording ("Entwicklungsteam") over forms that need special characters.

## Links

Link text says where the link goes ("the pricing page"), never "here". Link the
primary source for every claim that needs one, at the claim, not in a list at the end.
