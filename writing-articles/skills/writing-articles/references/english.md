# English

Rules for articles written in English, on top of `style.md`'s general rules.
When a project publishes both languages, write the English version as English, not as
a translation of the German one: the facts, numbers, sources, and structure match, the
sentences do not have to.

## Spelling and variety

- Use the variety the context file names. If it names none, use the one the existing
  English posts use; if there are none, use American spelling and say so in the report.
- Keep one variety per article: color or colour, license or licence, not both.

## Address and voice

- "You" for the reader, "I" for a single author. No corporate "we" for one person.
- Contractions are fine in a casual article ("it's", "you'll") and should be used
  consistently. A formal article avoids them.

## Headings and titles

- Sentence case for headings and titles: "How to self-host n8n", not "How To Self-Host
  N8n". Product names keep their own casing.
- A heading states the content or asks the reader's question. Avoid the "Topic: what it
  does" template on every heading.

## Typography

- Straight or curly quotes, whichever the site already uses, but consistently.
- No em dashes as general punctuation, and no spaced hyphen or en dash as a pause. Use
  a period, a comma, a colon, or parentheses. An unspaced en dash in a number range in
  a table is fine.
- Oxford comma as the context file says; if silent, follow the existing posts.

## Numbers, dates, units

- Decimal point, comma for thousands: 3.5 GB, 10,000 requests.
- A space between number and unit (4 GB); none before % (20%).
- Currency symbol before the number: €5, $1,790.
- Dates: 2 December 2026 (British) or December 2, 2026 (American), matching the
  variety. Never 02/12/2026 in running text, which readers parse differently.

## Translated terms from German sources

- Name German laws by their official English title where one exists, with the German
  abbreviation once: "the Accessibility Strengthening Act (BFSG)".
- Keep German authority and court names, with a short English gloss on first use.
- Convert German number formatting (3,5 to 3.5) and quote marks; never the values.

## Machine-written patterns in English

The list in `style.md` applies in full. The words that show up most in English
drafts: "delve", "leverage", "robust", "seamless", "pivotal", "tapestry",
"cutting-edge", "unlock", "navigate the landscape", "it's worth noting", "in
conclusion", "here's the thing". One is a question; several in an article are a
finding.
