# Disclosing paid links

Not legal advice. When the context file names a market, follow that market's rule and
the site's existing labelling; when in doubt, use the stricter option.

## Every market

- Label each paid link at the link, not only in a footer or on an about page.
- Put a short notice before the first paid link that explains the label in plain words:
  the author earns a commission, the reader pays nothing extra.
- Mark recommendations that pay nothing as such, so the reader can tell the two apart.
- Paid links carry `rel="sponsored"` (with `nofollow` if the site adds it). Prefer the
  site's redirect or component over a raw tracking URL in the text.
- A free product or a discount for the author is a material connection too; say so.
- The recommendation must be honest: the author would make it without the commission.

## Germany and Austria

Commercial intent must be recognizable (§ 5a UWG in Germany). Courts have found
"Affiliate-Link", "sponsored by", or an unexplained symbol not clear enough.

- Safest: "Werbung" or "Anzeige" directly at the link, or "(Werbelink)".
- An asterisk at the link is common but not settled law. Use it only together with a
  notice at the top of the article, visible without scrolling, that explains it. A
  footnote at the end of the page is not enough.
- Social posts with paid links start with "Werbung" or "Anzeige".
- Affiliate tracking may set cookies after the click; the site's privacy policy needs
  a section on affiliate links.

## United States

The FTC asks for a clear and conspicuous disclosure close to the recommendation and
before the link. Plain words such as "paid link" or "I earn a commission if you buy"
work; hashtags or "affiliate" alone may not be understood. In video, say it out loud
and show it.

## United Kingdom

The CMA asks that the commercial relationship be obvious upfront; "Ad" or "Advert"
labels are the clear option.
